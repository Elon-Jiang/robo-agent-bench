# Hard manipulation tasks: evidence through 7 November 2026

## Reading the evidence

**Recommendation:** prioritize contact alignment under occlusion, articulated multi-step operations, and recovery under physical perturbations. Deformables and granular transfer offer useful coverage gaps, but weaker comparative evidence of persistent difficulty.

“Best” below means the highest verified result **within the explicitly named comparison/protocol**, not an exhaustive worldwide record. A universal November task-by-task leaderboard does not exist; global maxima are **UNVERIFIED** unless a bounded official-board maximum is identified. Rows marked **snapshot** locate weaknesses in a particular evaluation, not universally hardest tasks. Rates are full execution success unless explicitly labeled otherwise. Explanations are mechanism-based inferences unless attributed to the authors. Different sensing, training, retry budgets, and success checkers prevent pooling rates.

## Difficulty factors ranked by consistency of evidence

Ordinal synthesis, not a statistical meta-analysis; evidence strength includes contradictory results.

| Rank | Factor | Cross-benchmark evidence and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contact geometry, orientation, narrow tolerances | CaP/FAEA peg failures, CodeActionBench scanning/hanging, RoboTwin mug hanging, RLBench shape sorting. Strongest repeated pattern; RLBench peg success shows controller and geometry dependence. Sources above. |
| 2 | Sequential operations with articulation and recovery | Drawer/microwave tasks recur in SimplerEnv, RoboTwin, LIBERO, RoboCasa; CALVIN and BEHAVIOR expose compounding sequences. Recovery is a plausible mechanism, not independently isolated by every benchmark. |
| 3 | Generalization under observation/state perturbations | RoboTwin's clean→random ranking and LIBERO variants support this; learned skills can substantially close gaps. Joint contact/friction/occlusion changes deserve more study than appearance alone. |
| 4 | Non-prehensile/tool-mediated interaction | CodeActionBench orientation/scan operations, Meta-World sweep, ManipBench shooting/sweeping. Evidence is heterogeneous; offline accuracy cannot establish online failure rates. |
| 5 | Bimanual coordination | Historically difficult in CaP, explicitly covered in CodeActionBench/RoboTwin; ASPIRE's handover result weakens claims of persistent low ceilings. |
| 6 | Deformables and liquids | ManipBench rope probes reasoning, not execution. Sparse comparable agent results make persistent difficulty **UNVERIFIED**. Treat as an evaluation gap, not the strongest demonstrated failure factor. |

**Under-covered specifically by agents:** controlled recovery after slips/jams; partially observed contact and active wrist-camera inspection; coupling articulation with obstruction clearing; deformation/topology and mass-conserving transfer. This is an inference from the surveyed task sets, not proof of absence. Generic insertion, tools, and bimanual manipulation already have coverage. The opportunity is their **combination with hidden state, perturbation, and auditable recovery**.

Benchmark validity matters: a later audit reports mismatches between LIBERO-PRO task-perturbation instructions and checkers. Validate instruction–goal consistency before interpreting failures. [Audit](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21650).

## Proposed MuJoCo tabletop tasks

Design proposals, not demonstrated performance claims. Use one arm and passive fixtures; validate reach, gripper width, and feasible orientations on the chosen SO-101/YAM model. `goto`/`move_by` must support the orientations needed; otherwise redesign fixtures. Keep only `grasp/place/goto/move_by/sweep`, wrist RGB, and ordinary proprioception; no oracle object poses or task-solving macros.

| Task | Description and difficulty | Programmatic success | Simulation feasibility risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Occluded keyed connector** | Retrieve a keyed plug, inspect its underside, regrasp against a fixture, and insert behind a lip. Requires active perception, orientation correction, and recovery from shallow jams. | Plug–socket depth and angular tolerance, released retention after settling, no prohibited collisions or excessive contact load. | High: small clearances and contact solver settings can create artificial jams. Chamfer geometry, calibrate friction, and verify same-primitives oracle trajectories across seeds. |
| **Unlatch, clear, load, close** | Release a drawer latch, remove a loose obstruction, place a designated object inside, then close and relatch. Hidden mechanical state makes blindly repeating a pull ineffective. | Recorded valid latch transition, obstruction outside the slide path, target contained, drawer closed and latch engaged simultaneously. | Medium: model a physical latch and slider rather than a magical scripted unlock. Reject unreachable latch/handle combinations and penetration-based shortcuts. |
| **Hook retrieval followed by hanging** | Fetch a hook, pull a mug from beyond direct grasp reach, park the tool, regrasp the mug, and hang it. Require adaptation after a recoverable tool slip. | Mug reaches retrieval region through tool contact, then hangs released with handle around the peg and sustained support; tool safely parked. | Medium: compound collision geometry must preserve handle openings; collision meshes can accidentally seal them. Ensure the tool extends reach without making the arm workspace impossible. |
| **Cable routing around guides** | Straighten a loose cable and feed its end through a guide while routing around posts. Grasp location changes the whole shape; local progress can create tangles elsewhere. | Endpoint in socket region, centerline passes required guide surfaces in order, correct winding around posts, no prohibited crossings; stable after release. | High: linked capsules or flex elements need calibrated bending, self-contact, and gripper friction. Topology checks need hysteresis near boundaries. MuJoCo supports deformable modeling, but fidelity needs validation. [Documentation](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/modeling.html#deformable-objects). |
| **Granular spill recovery and transfer** | Grasp a dustpan, sweep spilled granules onto it, transfer into a narrow container, then recover residual spills. Couples tool placement, friction, containment, and stopping decisions. | Count particle centers fully inside the target, penalize off-table particles and residual spill, require tools released and container upright after settling. | High: many contacts increase compute; tiny particles can tunnel or stick. Use resolved rigid granules and call it granular transfer—not liquid simulation. Validate conservation and contact convergence. |

Score full terminal success separately from subgoal progress. Compare clean runs with seeded, recoverable disturbances and report conditional recovery. Fix tool-call/time budgets, expose sensing assumptions, hold out geometry/friction combinations, and verify physical solvability before counting failures. Start with the connector, drawer, and hook tasks; treat cable and granules as feasibility prototypes.
